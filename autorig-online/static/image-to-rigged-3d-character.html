One picture in, a whole character out

Most image-to-3D tools stop at a mesh. You get a static model, and rigging it is still your evening. AutoRig treats the picture as the start of the whole pipeline: the image becomes a textured 3D model, the model gets a skeleton and skin weights, and an animation library is retargeted onto it. All of it arrives as a single task with a single progress page.

The routing decision is made once, right after upload. The site looks at your picture and decides whether it shows a character that can carry a skeleton. If it does, you get generation plus rigging. If it does not — a vehicle, a prop, a building, a character whose limbs are fused into the body — you still get a proper PBR 3D model, because the model is what you paid for either way.

How it works, step by step

Four stages run without you touching anything after the upload.

  1. Drop the picture

    Open the AutoRig home page and drop a PNG, JPG or WEBP file on the upload zone. One image is one character. You need to be signed in, because generation is charged to an account.

  2. The site decides what it is looking at

    Detection checks whether the image shows a character in a T-pose or A-pose with limbs clear of the torso. This decides the route only: riggable pictures go to generation plus rigging, everything else goes to generation alone.

  3. The 3D model is generated

    The picture goes to the GPU farm and comes back as a textured mesh with PBR materials, retopologised into usable geometry rather than a raw scan-like surface.

  4. The model is rigged and animated

    A skeleton is fitted, skin weights are solved, and the animation library is retargeted onto the new character. The task then publishes GLB, Unity FBX, the HDRP unitypackage, Blender files and a preview video.

What makes a picture riggable

The detector is looking for a readable body, not for art quality. A rough sketch in a clean T-pose beats a beautiful render of a character folded into a dramatic action pose.

  • The character faces the camera, standing upright and roughly centred.
  • Arms are away from the torso - straight out for a T-pose, or angled down for an A-pose.
  • Legs are separated, with a visible gap between them.
  • The whole body is in frame, including hands and feet.
  • The background is plain, or at least does not blend into the silhouette.

And when the picture is not a character

Nothing is rejected. A picture that cannot carry a skeleton still produces a textured PBR 3D model - you simply get the model without the skeleton and the animations. That is a routing decision made per picture, never a failure message.

Several pictures at once

Drop a handful of images and each one becomes its own task, queued together. The upload zone accepts a multi-file selection and starts them in sequence, so a set of concept sheets can turn into a set of rigged characters in one pass. Drop a single file and you land on that task's page; drop several and you land on your task list.

Questions people actually ask

Do I need a 3D model to start?

No. That is the point of this page - a single picture is enough. If you already have a GLB, FBX or OBJ, upload it directly instead and skip generation entirely.

Does the character have to be in a T-pose?

A T-pose or an A-pose both work, as long as the limbs are clear of the body. The pose decides whether rigging is attempted; it never decides whether you get a model.

How long does it take?

The examples on this page took roughly 20 to 50 minutes end to end, including time spent waiting in the farm queue. The task page shows live progress, and you can close the tab - the task keeps running.

What if I upload something that is not a character?

You get a textured PBR 3D model of whatever it was. Detection routes the job, it does not reject it.

Can I rig several characters at once?

Yes. Select several images in the upload zone and each becomes its own task, charged separately.

Do I need an account?

For generating a character from a picture, yes - Unlimited Monthly is linked to your account. Uploading an existing 3D model for rigging remains available without a subscription.
